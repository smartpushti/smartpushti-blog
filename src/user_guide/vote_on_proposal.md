## How to Vote on a Proposal

Voting allows you to evaluate proposals and help decide how AROGYA incentives are distributed.

---

## Step 1: Navigate to Voting Page

Go to:

**Dashboard → Vote on Proposal**

This is where you can submit your vote.

---

## Step 2: What You Need Before Voting

To vote, you must have:

* A **Proposal ID** (the proposal you want to vote on)
* A **Conviction ID** (your locked stake used for voting)
* An **active conviction** (not expired)
* The proposal must still be within its **active voting period**

You cannot vote once the voting window has closed.

---

## Step 3: Submit Your Vote

When voting, you will:

1. Enter the **Proposal ID**
2. Select your **Conviction ID**
3. Choose a **score between 0–10**
4. Submit your vote

Your voting power depends on your conviction level (lock duration).

---

## Step 4: Voting Rules

* You can vote only **once per proposal** using a conviction.
* You cannot use the same conviction to vote twice on the same proposal.
* Higher conviction = stronger voting weight.

---

## Step 5: Vote Fairly and Responsibly

Always evaluate proposals carefully before voting.

Consider whether:

* The work is genuine and clearly documented
* The contribution benefits the ecosystem
* The effort matches the requested incentives
* Evidence and progress are verifiable

---

## Step 6: Types of Contributions to Evaluate

Proposals may fall into different categories, such as:

* **Research & Academia** — scientific studies, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, data analysis, technical papers, and research replication
* **Education & Knowledge Sharing** — tutorials, educational guides, courses, workshops, explainers, and evidence-based health information
* **Health & Wellness Innovation** — development of new tools, technologies, protocols, interventions, or approaches that could improve health and wellbeing
* **Public Health** — disease prevention, health promotion, community health initiatives, screening programs, and population-level interventions
* **Mental Health & Wellbeing** — research, education, interventions, peer support, and resources related to psychological wellbeing and mental health
* **Nutrition & Lifestyle** — evidence-based work on nutrition, physical activity, sleep, stress management, healthy lifestyles, and preventive health
* **Traditional & Complementary Health** — rigorous research and documentation of traditional, complementary, and alternative health practices, particularly where evidence and safety can be evaluated
* **Community Health & Outreach** — initiatives that improve access to health information, resources, services, or support within communities
* **Open Science & Data** — open datasets, research infrastructure, reproducible research, open-source tools, and resources that enable further health research
* **Peer Review & Scientific Criticism** — reviewing research, identifying methodological problems, reproducing findings, and improving the quality of published work
* **Health Communication** — translating scientific evidence into accessible content for researchers, practitioners, patients, and the general public
* **Collaboration & Research Networks** — projects that connect researchers, practitioners, educators, and communities to conduct or support health and wellness research
* **Policy & Evidence-Based Advocacy** — research and analysis that can inform health policies, public programs, and institutional decision-making
* **Practical Community Impact** — measurable initiatives that apply existing evidence to improve health outcomes or wellbeing in real-world communities

Each category may require different evaluation standards.

---

Remember that if the conviction-weighted mean score is between +1 and +5, the proposal will receive incentives.

___

## Important Tips

* Vote honestly and based on quality.
* Avoid bias or personal influence.
* Responsible voting strengthens the ecosystem.
